What you should be able to do after this lecture

  1. Classify adversarial ML attacks along the five dimensions of the NIST AI 100-2 taxonomy.
  2. Distinguish availability poisoning, targeted poisoning, and backdoor attacks by mechanism and detectability.
  3. Explain why clean-label and backdoor attacks evade standard validation metrics.
  4. Implement a PGD evasion attack on tabular data and measure its disparate impact by race.
  5. Simulate a label-flip poisoning loop and plot AUC and AIR degradation as a function of poison rate.
  6. Build a shadow-model membership inference pipeline and interpret the resulting ROC curve.

Potential Questions for In-Class Quiz

Short-answer (7-10 minutes each). Justify claims.

Quiz 1: PGD Evasion and Disparate Impact

Based on the lab results, is a model that is "more fair under attack" actually safer to deploy? Distinguish between absolute harm and relative disparity in your answer.

Quiz 2: Data Poisoning and the Stealth Zone

Using the results from the lab, identify the stealth zone — the poison rate range where the attack is undetectable by standard AUC monitoring yet causes a meaningful fairness violation.

Quiz 3: Membership Inference and the Overfitting–Privacy Link

If this model were deployed by a county probation office with a public API, what specific information could a journalist extract using this attack? Be precise about what "membership" reveals in this context.
